01 · The problem

Good engineering stories stall between the design doc and the blog.

i.

Stuck in a doc

The design doc already has the story. Turning it into a post takes a week of evenings, so it waits for a quiet sprint that never comes.

ii.

Review by comment thread

Engineering checks the facts, legal checks the names, marketing checks the tone. Each pass is another round of comments on another copy.

iii.

One hostname too many

Posts about real systems carry real details: a customer's name, an internal host, a latency figure nobody signed off. One slip and the post comes down.

03 · The check

Catch the internal detail before the internet does.

DevRel teams write about real systems. Every draft is read against the author's notes and the post's list of approved names before a reviewer sees it.

  • Company and product names that aren't on the approved list
  • Internal hostnames, IP addresses and service URLs
  • Secrets: API keys, tokens, private keys, database URLs with passwords
  • Security weaknesses in systems that may still be live
  • Numbers that don't appear in the author's notes
  • Personal data and shaky claims about other products

High-severity findings block the post until they're fixed. Medium and low findings need a person to confirm them. Change the draft and it needs a fresh check.

08 · Questions

Asked and answered.

Which blogs does Masthead work with?

Any blog whose posts are MDX files in a git repository: Next.js, Astro, Vite or your own build. You tell Masthead where posts and thumbnails live and which components the blog has.

Does Masthead push to our main branch?

No. Each post arrives as a pull request on its own branch. Protected branches stay protected, and you merge when you're ready.

Why do we bring our own Anthropic key?

So model usage is billed to you by Anthropic at Anthropic's rates, with nothing added. Masthead charges per seat.

What does the confidentiality check look for?

Company and product names outside the post's approved list, internal hostnames and URLs, secrets, security weaknesses, personal data, and numbers that aren't in the author's notes. It also compiles the MDX.

Can the check miss something?

Yes. It combines a model review with pattern checks for secrets and internal hosts, and neither is perfect. It catches the common slips early so your reviewers can spend their time on the post itself.

Will drafts sound like our blog?

Drafts follow your shared voice guide, your component guide and a reference post you choose. Writers edit every outline and draft before anything is checked or published.

Does Masthead post to LinkedIn and X for us?

No. It writes the LinkedIn post, X thread and newsletter blurb for each post. You copy them and post them yourself.
